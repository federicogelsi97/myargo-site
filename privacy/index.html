Argo Privacy Policy

Last updated: 13 September 2026

Argo is an app that helps you understand your dog. This page says what Argo collects, where it goes, and how to delete it. Argo is a small project run by its founders from Italy. For anything on this page, write to info@myargo.app.

1. What Argo collects

When you sign in (the first thing you do in Argo)

  • With Apple: Apple gives Argo a user identifier and your email address, or a private relay address if you choose "Hide My Email". Argo never sees your Apple password and asks Apple for nothing else, not even your name.
  • With email: your email address, to send you a sign-in link and service messages. Never sold, never used for advertising.

While you use Argo

  • A photo of your dog and your answers to six questions (name, breed, age band, sex and neutering, how long your dog has been with you, up to three behaviours). Used to write the Dog Report and the shareable card, and to give every answer in the chat. The photo is stored in a private folder that only your account can open.
  • Your dog's profile, record, and diary: everything you add about your dog, including photos, health entries, weight, documents, and notes.
  • Your conversations with Argo, so that Argo remembers your dog over time.
  • Your name, a short line about you and your photo, only if you add them in Settings. They show who is who in your dog's family.
  • A random installation identifier for your device, to stop scripts from generating thousands of answers. It is not your name, email, or advertising ID.
  • Your location, only while the map is open. Argo asks your phone where you are to show the vets, pet shops and dog parks around you. The coordinates go to OpenStreetMap's Overpass service to find those places. If you turn on "Dog sitters" on the map (it starts off), on iPhone the centre of the area being searched, not your exact position, also goes to Apple Maps to find dog sitters nearby; Apple sends back their name, position and phone number. Argo does not store them, does not send them to its own servers, and does not send them to the AI. If you ask for directions, Argo opens the maps app you choose with the address of the place, not with your position.
  • Usage events, such as "report shown", "screen opened", "message sent", with the name of the screen, how many seconds you spent on it, the app version and the type of device. A health answer counts with its urgency level (green, yellow or red), never with its words. Events never contain your photos, your messages, your dog's name, your location or your email. Before you sign in they are tied to a random identifier generated on your phone; after, to your account's identifier, which is a code and not your name.
  • Crash and performance reports. If Argo crashes, or a screen is slow to open, a report goes to Sentry with the app version and the update in use, the phone model and system, the screen you were on and the screens before it, and your account's identifier. Never your messages, your dog's name, your email, your IP address or the name of your phone. The same happens for errors inside our server functions.
  • A notification address for your phone, only if you allow notifications and share a dog with someone. It lets our server tell your phone that a dose has already been given, so the reminder goes quiet. It carries no name, no dog, and no content.

When you share your dog

  • Family. If you invite someone with a code, they see everything about that dog that you add: profile, record, diary, memory, reminders. They can add and change things too. Your conversations with Argo stay yours: nobody else in the family reads them. Photos stay in the private folder of the person who added them. Anyone can leave; nobody can remove anyone else. If you add your name, a short line and a photo, only people who share at least one dog with you see them, and they stop seeing them when you no longer share a dog. Only you can change them. They are deleted with your account, and they are never sent to the AI.
  • Emergency card. From the record you can create an emergency card: a web link with your dog's name, photo, vaccines, treatments, current medications, the notes on character, diet and known conditions you write for your dog, and the contact you type in. Anyone with the link can open it, without signing in. You can revoke it in the app at any time. Share it only with people who look after your dog.

Argo does not ask for tracking permission and does not track you across other apps.

2. Argo uses AI

Argo's answers are written by an artificial intelligence model. To produce them, the photo of your dog, your answers, your dog's record, and your chat messages are sent to Anthropic, PBC (United States). Argo cannot work without this step, so it is part of the service you sign up for; a screen inside the app tells you before the first time it happens. Anthropic processes this data on our behalf under a data processing agreement with the European Union's Standard Contractual Clauses, deletes inputs and outputs within 30 days, and does not use them to train its models. Your email address is never sent to the AI provider; your dog's name is, because Argo uses it in its answers. Every request carries your sign-in token, so that our server can count usage per account and stop abuse.

3. Where your data lives

Your account, your dog's record, photos, and conversations are stored in the United States (Virginia), hosted by Supabase. Usage events are stored in the European Union, hosted by PostHog, which is set not to keep your IP address and not to derive your location from it. Crash and performance reports are stored in the European Union (Frankfurt), hosted by Sentry. The AI processing in section 2 takes place in the United States.

Argo is built for dog owners everywhere, starting with the United States, and runs on one database rather than one per continent. If you are in the European Union or the United Kingdom, your data leaves that area. It does so under contract: storing it is part of the service you sign up for, and Supabase processes it on our behalf under a data processing agreement that includes the European Union's Standard Contractual Clauses. You can ask for a copy at info@myargo.app.

We do not sell your data. The free version of Argo may show advertising inside a conversation. To pick an ad, the ad network may receive the text of the conversation where the ad appears, your IP address, your device model, and the installation identifier. It never receives your email, your dog's record, your photos, or any health conversation: ads never appear there, and nothing from a health conversation ever reaches an ad network.

5. Argo is not a vet

Argo gives general information about dogs. It does not diagnose and does not prescribe. On health topics it tells you how urgent something looks and always points you to a vet. Health conversations are free and never show advertising.

7. Deleting your account

Open Argo, go to Settings, and choose Delete account. Argo deletes every photo, then your account, your email, your dog's record, and every conversation from our systems, immediately. Copies at the AI provider expire within 30 days. You do not need to write to us or explain why.

If you share a dog with other people, deleting your account does not delete the dog for them: it passes to the member who has been there longest, with its record, diary and memory. Two things go with you: your conversations with Argo, and the photos you added, so the dog stays with them without those pictures. A dog nobody else shares is deleted with you. The same hand-over happens if you leave a shared dog from the family screen, or start again with it: the others keep the dog. If you only want to stop looking after a shared dog, use Leave instead of deleting your account.

8. Your rights

If you are in the European Union or the United Kingdom, you have the right to access, correct, export, and delete your data, and to object to some uses. Most of this you can do inside the app; for the rest, write to info@myargo.app and we answer within 30 days. You can also complain to your data protection authority. Our legal bases: the contract with you (the service, including the AI step and the storage in section 3), our legitimate interest in running a safe service (abuse limits, usage events and counters without content), and your consent for anything optional you can switch off.

If you are in the United States, you have similar rights under the laws of your state. Argo does not sell personal data and does not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising.

9. Children

Argo is not for children under 16. If you believe a child has created an account, write to info@myargo.app and we delete it.

10. Security and this website

Data is encrypted in transit and at rest. Keys for our providers never leave our servers. If we learn of a breach affecting your data, we tell you and the authority as the law requires. myargo.app sets no cookies and runs no analytics; it loads its fonts from Google Fonts, and nothing else from third parties.

11. Changes and contact

When this page changes in a way that matters, Argo tells you inside the app before the change takes effect. Contact: info@myargo.app.
